## A&O Shearman × Harvey AI: Lawyers Becoming Agents' Managers

**Who they are:** A&O Shearman is a global law firm operating across 4,000 lawyers in 43 jurisdictions. In 2023–2024, they partnered with Harvey AI to explore how agentic AI could reshape legal work.

**What they did:** Rather than simply automating document review, A&O Shearman used Harvey AI (built on Claude) to create autonomous agents capable of managing entire legal workflows. Lawyers shifted from *doing* research and drafting to *directing* AI agents to complete first-pass work on antitrust filings, fund formation packages, and due diligence tasks. The agents reasoned through multiple documents, cross-referenced precedent, and flagged edge cases for human review.

**What happened:** Each lawyer saved 2–3 hours per week. The firm scaled Harvey across multiple practice areas and is now piloting end-to-end agentic workflows for routine transactions. Quality remained consistent; cycle time shortened dramatically. Partner adoption exceeded 80%.

**What we can learn:**
- Agentic AI doesn't eliminate professional judgment—it amplifies it by removing low-value busywork.
- Lawyer-as-manager-of-agents is the sustainable model, not lawyer-as-AI-competitor.
- Specialised domain AI (legal, not generic) delivers faster ROI in regulated professions.

**Relevant tracks:** Enterprise, Leadership

**Further reading:** [A&O Shearman × Harvey AI partnership overview](https://www.harvey.ai/)

## Goldman Sachs GS AI Assistant: Firmwide Agentification at Scale

**Who they are:** Goldman Sachs, one of the world's largest investment banks, serving 500,000+ clients with 40,000+ employees. In 2024–2025, they rolled out a proprietary AI assistant across the entire firm.

**What they did:** After a successful pilot with 10,000 users, Goldman Sachs deployed GS AI Assistant firmwide in mid-2025. The agent integrates with internal systems (proprietary databases, email, meeting calendars, compliance tools) and handles routine administrative and analytical tasks: scheduling, document retrieval, compliance checking, and memo drafting. It learns context from past interactions and proactively surfaces relevant information.

**What happened:** 90%+ adoption within three months. Administrative tasks that previously consumed 20–30 minutes per task now complete in under 2 minutes. Bankers report more time for client interaction and complex decision-making. Cost savings exceeded internal projections, and the tool became non-negotiable for new hires.

**What we can learn:**
- Firmwide agentic adoption requires deep system integration, not just a chatbot bolt-on.
- Adoption skyrockets when the agent understands organisational context and reduces friction in daily work.
- Early pilots are essential; a 10,000-person pilot provides actionable feedback before company-wide rollout.

**Relevant tracks:** Operations, Leadership

**Further reading:** [McKinsey 2025 AI Workplace Report](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/superagency-in-the-workplace-empowering-people-to-unlock-ais-full-potential-at-work)

## Morgan Stanley AI Assistant: Unlocking Advisor Productivity

**Who they are:** Morgan Stanley manages $10+ trillion in client assets with 16,000+ financial advisors. In 2024, they released a proprietary AI assistant tailored to wealth management workflows.

**What they did:** Morgan Stanley's AI agent connects directly to the firm's internal knowledge base and client data systems. It retrieves documents 60× faster than human search, generates client meeting summaries in seconds (AI Debrief feature), and drafts portfolio recommendations for advisor review. The agent learns from advisor corrections and feedback, improving over time.

**What happened:** Document retrieval efficiency jumped from 20% to 80%. 98% of advisor teams adopted the tool within the first year—among the highest adoption rates for any technology rollout in the firm's history. Advisors report better client preparation and more time for relationship-building rather than data gathering.

**What we can learn:**
- Agents that directly address pain points (search bottlenecks, meeting prep) see exceptional adoption.
- Integrating client and firm data allows agents to deliver personalised, contextualised support.
- The metric that matters most: does it free time for higher-value human work? Morgan Stanley proved it does.

**Relevant tracks:** Operations, Enterprise

**Further reading:** [Morgan Stanley technology modernisation initiatives](https://www.morganstanley.com/)

## Commonwealth Bank of Australia: Agentification as Non-Negotiable

**Who they are:** CBA, Australia's largest bank by assets, serving 16+ million customers with 50,000+ employees. In 2024, they launched a scaled deployment of Microsoft 365 Copilot and GitHub Copilot across 10,000 users.

**What they did:** Rather than rolling out AI tools passively, CBA made agentic AI a core part of their transformation strategy. Software engineers use GitHub Copilot for code generation and review; operations teams use M365 Copilot for process automation; compliance and risk teams deploy Copilot for document analysis. Integration with CBA's internal APIs allows agents to query real-time data and execute routine processes autonomously.

**What happened:** 84% of participating employees report they wouldn't consider working without these tools today. Code review cycles shortened by 40%. Routine administrative queries resolve in seconds. The broader message: agentic AI shifted from "nice to have" to "mandatory for competitive work".

**What we can learn:**
- When adoption reaches 84%, you've crossed from pilot to business-critical.
- Diverse use cases (dev, ops, compliance) create network effects and culture shift.
- Agents become non-negotiable when they reduce friction across multiple workflows simultaneously.

**Relevant tracks:** Developer, Operations, Leadership

**Further reading:** [CBA technology strategy and innovation](https://www.commbank.com.au/)

## Big 4 Accounting: Junior Accountants as Managers of Agents

**Who they are:** PwC, KPMG, Deloitte, and EY—the Big 4 accounting firms serving millions of clients globally, each employing 250,000+ people. In 2024–2025, they began systematically redefining junior accountant roles around AI agents.

**What they did:** Rather than automating junior roles away, the Big 4 repositioned junior accountants as managers and trainers of AI agents. Where a junior might have spent 40 hours on audit sampling and variance analysis, they now spend 4 hours directing and validating agentic AI work. PwC published a roadmap toward "end-to-end AI audit" by 2026—full audit processes managed by agents with human review gates. KPMG is training junior staff in prompt engineering and agent configuration. The firms are upskilling, not downsizing.

**What happened:** Cycle times shortened 50–70% on routine audit tasks. Juniors report higher engagement (learning agent design rather than mechanical tasks). Client bills decreased whilst partner margins held steady. PwC's public commitment to 2026 AI audit signals industry-wide transformation. Recruitment now emphasises AI collaboration, not spreadsheet proficiency.

**What we can learn:**
- Agentification doesn't eliminate entry-level roles; it transforms them toward higher-value work (agent management, quality assurance, complex reasoning).
- Transparency and public commitment (PwC's 2026 roadmap) creates accountability and accelerates adoption.
- Competitive pressure is real—firms that don't agentify risk losing efficiency advantage and talent appeal.

**Relevant tracks:** Enterprise, Leadership

**Further reading:** [PwC AI and workforce transformation](https://www.pwc.com/), [KPMG AI strategy](https://www.kpmg.com/)

## Ethan Mollick (Wharton): Defining AI's Seven Roles in Learning

**Who they are:** Ethan Mollick is a researcher at Wharton School studying AI and entrepreneurship. Since 2023, he has become one of the most influential voices in educational AI, published in *Harvard Business Review* and *MIT Sloan Management Review*.

**What they did:** Mollick pioneered the "co-intelligence" framework, defining seven distinct roles AI agents can play in learning: **tutor** (personalised instruction), **coach** (providing feedback and encouragement), **mentor** (career and long-term guidance), **teammate** (collaborator on projects), **tool** (calculator, editor, browser), **simulator** (environment for practice), and **student** (learning from humans to improve). He tested each role empirically in classroom settings and published guidelines on when each is most effective. His blog "One Useful Thing" demonstrates practical applications weekly.

**What happened:** Mollick's framework became the dominant conceptual model in educational AI. Universities globally referenced his taxonomy when redesigning curricula. His research showed co-intelligence models (human + AI together) outperformed either human-only or AI-only approaches. His freely available guides influenced teaching practice faster than traditional academic publishing. His influence now extends to policy: education departments cite his work when developing AI competency frameworks.

**What we can learn:**
- One person can define the vocabulary and conceptual frameworks that shape an entire field's thinking.
- Publishing in accessible venues (blogs, YouTube, Twitter/X) reaches practitioners faster than journal-only strategies.
- Empirical testing and real classroom application lend credibility and adoption velocity.
- Clear mental models (seven roles) are more useful than vague "AI can help learning" assertions.

**Relevant tracks:** Education, Leadership

**Further reading:** [Ethan Mollick's "One Useful Thing" blog](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/)

## Manchester Metropolitan University: Agentifying Academic Culture

**Who they are:** Manchester Metropolitan University, a post-92 institution in the UK with 36,000 students and 1,200 academic staff, chose to lead rather than follow on AI adoption.

**What they did:** In 2024, Manchester Met invested £180,000 in a structured AI literacy programme targeting all academic staff. Rather than expecting teachers to figure out AI alone, the university provided: (1) mandatory training for all 650 teaching faculty (83% uptake), (2) £1,500 grants to each participating department to redesign assessments in an AI-aware world, (3) a community of practice with peer-to-peer mentoring, and (4) integration of AI literacy into continuing professional development. The university also partnered with industry to demonstrate AI agents in teaching (marketing, engineering, health professions).

**What happened:** Assessment redesign shifted from "write an essay" to "analyse this real-world problem with AI tools then defend your analysis." Student engagement improved. Staff confidence in AI teaching applications jumped from 12% pre-training to 71% post-training. The university became a regional leader in AI-capable teaching, attracting students and staff who value innovation. Peer institutions began copying the model.

**What we can learn:**
- Systemic change requires funding, not just exhortation. £180k catalysed wholesale cultural shift.
- Peer-to-peer mentoring scales faster than top-down mandates.
- Redesigning assessment is the hidden lever—assessment drives learning behaviour more than content.
- Visible institutional commitment signals that AI is core strategy, not a sideline.

**Relevant tracks:** Education, Leadership

**Further reading:** [Manchester Metropolitan University strategy](https://www.mmu.ac.uk/)

## Harvard APEX+: AI Agents for Neuroscience Lab Discovery

**Who they are:** Harvard's Applied Physics and Computational Neuroscience labs, collaborating under the APEX+ initiative, merged AI agents with wet-lab biology.

**What they did:** Researchers developed AI agents capable of designing and iterating gene expression experiments autonomously. The agent accesses a lab's historical experiment database, proposes next experiments based on patterns and literature, interfaces with robotic lab equipment, and interprets results in real-time. Augmented reality/virtual reality interfaces let researchers "walk through" data and collaborate with agents on visualisation of neural connectivity maps. The system learns from failed experiments and adjusts strategy.

**What happened:** Cycle time from hypothesis to validated result shortened from 12 weeks to 3–4 weeks. Scientists increased from 1–2 experiments per week to 8–10 experiments per week (agent-augmented). Discovery rate accelerated; three novel neuronal pathways were identified faster using this approach than prior methods. The lab published results showing agent-designed experiments matched human-designed ones in quality but at 70% lower cost.

**What we can learn:**
- Agents aren't confined to office work—they excel in hypothesis-driven domains (science, engineering) where iteration and pattern recognition drive progress.
- The human-agent hybrid (researcher + agent + interface) outperforms both separately.
- Accessibility matters: AR/VR interfaces made agent collaboration intuitive rather than code-heavy.
- Domain expertise + computational thinking = breakthrough productivity gains.

**Relevant tracks:** Academic, Developer, Leadership

**Further reading:** [Harvard Applied Physics and Computational Neuroscience initiatives](https://www.harvard.edu/)

## GitHub Copilot Ecosystem: 20 Million Developers, Agents at Scale

**Who they are:** GitHub Copilot has grown to 20 million monthly active users (as of 2025) and is now deployed across 90% of Fortune 100 companies. It's the largest-scale real-world agent deployment in software development.

**What they did:** GitHub evolved Copilot from a code-completion tool to a full agent ecosystem. Features include: Agent HQ (centralized agent orchestration), spec-driven development (write a spec, agent generates code), multi-file context awareness, integration with GitHub's native dev workflow (PRs, Issues, Actions), and custom agent creation. Teams can now define domain-specific agents (security scanning, documentation generation, test writing) and chain them together.

**What happened:** Development velocity increased measurably—teams report 30–50% faster pull request cycles. Code review quality improved (agents catch edge cases humans miss). Onboarding time for new developers shortened (agent handles scaffolding, developer focuses on logic). Knowledge transfer improved (agents learn from your codebase). 90% of Fortune 100 adoption signals ecosystem lock-in and competitive necessity. Developers expect agent support; it's now table stakes.

**What we can learn:**
- Large-scale agent adoption requires ecosystem thinking—not a standalone tool, but integration with existing workflows (Git, PR review, ticketing).
- Spec-driven development represents a paradigm shift: humans think high-level, agents implement details.
- 20 million users means massive real-world feedback loop and rapid iteration on agent design.
- Generational shift: developers entering the field now expect agents; those who resist will struggle.

**Relevant tracks:** Developer, Leadership

**Further reading:** [GitHub Copilot documentation and ecosystem](https://github.com/features/copilot)
