# Proposal: Additional Tracks

## Current Tracks

- **Enterprise** — Governance, approved channels, trust architecture
- **Developer** — Interfaces, architecture, MCP tools, testing
- **Academic** — Literature, modular writing, peer review, reproducibility

## Proposed Additional Tracks

### 1. Creative Track

**Audience:** Writers, designers, marketers, content creators, artists exploring AI collaboration

**Why it's needed:** Creative work has distinct challenges — maintaining voice, iterating on subjective quality, copyright/attribution concerns, the "uncanny valley" of AI-generated content.

**Potential modules:**

| Module | Focus |
|--------|-------|
| **C1. Voice & Style** | Maintaining consistent voice, training AI on your style, style guides as specification |
| **C2. Creative Iteration** | Brainstorming with AI, when to constrain vs. explore, avoiding "AI-ish" outputs |
| **C3. Visual Collaboration** | Working with image generation, the description-to-visual gap, iterating on visual outputs |
| **C4. Attribution & Originality** | Copyright considerations, disclosure norms, what's "yours" when AI helped |

**Distinctive perspective:** Unlike other tracks, quality is inherently subjective. The verification question becomes "does this match my creative intent?" rather than "is this correct?"

---

### 2. Education Track

**Audience:** Teachers, trainers, instructional designers, tutors, curriculum developers

**Why it's needed:** Education has unique AI concerns — learning vs. outsourcing, assessment integrity, personalisation, the role of struggle in learning.

**Potential modules:**

| Module | Focus |
|--------|-------|
| **Ed1. AI-Resistant Assessment** | Designing assignments that develop skills AI can't shortcut |
| **Ed2. AI as Learning Partner** | When AI helps learning vs. replaces it, Socratic use patterns |
| **Ed3. Personalisation & Differentiation** | Using AI to adapt to learner needs without replacing teacher judgment |
| **Ed4. Teaching AI Literacy** | How to help others develop these skills, age-appropriate AI education |

**Distinctive perspective:** The goal isn't just using AI effectively — it's understanding when *not* to use it, and helping others make that judgment.

---

### 3. Operations Track

**Audience:** Project managers, operations staff, analysts, administrators, "knowledge workers" who aren't developers

**Why it's needed:** Most AI use happens in operational contexts — drafting, summarising, data wrangling, process documentation — but current tracks assume either technical depth (Developer) or formal governance roles (Enterprise).

**Potential modules:**

| Module | Focus |
|--------|-------|
| **O1. Document Workflows** | Drafting, editing, summarising, formatting — the daily work |
| **O2. Data Wrangling** | Cleaning, transforming, analysing data without code |
| **O3. Process Documentation** | Using AI to document, explain, and improve processes |
| **O4. Meeting & Communication** | Agendas, summaries, follow-ups, email drafting |

**Distinctive perspective:** Practical, task-oriented. Less theory, more "here's how to do X effectively."

---

### 4. Leadership Track

**Audience:** Executives, directors, team leads, decision-makers responsible for AI adoption

**Why it's needed:** Leaders need to make decisions about AI without necessarily using it deeply themselves. Strategy, investment, culture, risk.

**Potential modules:**

| Module | Focus |
|--------|-------|
| **L1. Strategic Assessment** | Where AI creates value, where it doesn't, avoiding hype |
| **L2. Building AI-Ready Teams** | Skills to hire for, culture shifts, training investment |
| **L3. Risk & Reputation** | What can go wrong, how to prepare, incident response |
| **L4. Vendor & Build Decisions** | Buy vs. build, evaluating AI products, avoiding lock-in |

**Distinctive perspective:** Decision-making and delegation rather than hands-on use. "What questions should I be asking?" rather than "How do I do this?"

---

### 5. Healthcare Track (Specialised)

**Audience:** Clinicians, healthcare administrators, health informaticists, medical researchers

**Why it's needed:** Healthcare has unique constraints — regulatory (HIPAA, GDPR), safety-critical, evidence-based practice norms, patient trust.

**Potential modules:**

| Module | Focus |
|--------|-------|
| **H1. Clinical Decision Support** | AI as advisor not decider, documentation, liability |
| **H2. Patient Data & Privacy** | What can/can't go into AI, de-identification, audit requirements |
| **H3. Evidence & Accuracy** | Medical hallucinations are dangerous, verification for clinical content |
| **H4. Patient Communication** | AI-assisted communication, maintaining empathy, disclosure |

**Distinctive perspective:** Safety-critical framing throughout. "First, do no harm" applied to AI use.

---

## Recommendations

### Highest Priority (Broadest Audience)

1. **Creative Track** — Large audience, distinct challenges, currently underserved
2. **Operations Track** — Where most actual AI use happens, practical focus

### Medium Priority (Specific but Significant)

3. **Education Track** — Important societal implications, growing concern
4. **Leadership Track** — Influences organisational adoption at scale

### Lower Priority (Narrower Audience)

5. **Healthcare Track** — Important but specialised; might work better as a modified Academic track with healthcare examples

---

## Implementation Notes

- Each track should be 3-4 modules (matching existing tracks)
- Could be developed incrementally — start with highest priority
- Some content could be shared/cross-referenced (e.g., verification appears in multiple tracks)
- Consider: should Operations be "core" rather than a track? It's potentially the most universal use case.
