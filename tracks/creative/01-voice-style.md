# C1. Voice & Style

## The Voice Problem

AI can write fluently. But can it write like *you*?

The default AI voice is:
- Competent but generic
- Helpful but bland
- Correct but characterless

For creative work, this isn't good enough. Your voice is your brand, your identity, your connection to readers.

## What Is Voice?

Voice is the distinctive quality that makes writing recognisably yours:

| Element | What It Includes |
|---------|------------------|
| **Diction** | Word choices, vocabulary level, jargon use |
| **Rhythm** | Sentence length variation, pacing, flow |
| **Tone** | Formal/informal, serious/playful, warm/distant |
| **Perspective** | How you see and frame things |
| **Quirks** | Distinctive habits, pet phrases, structural preferences |

Voice isn't just *what* you say — it's *how* you say it.

## Training AI on Your Voice

### The Style Reference Approach

Provide examples of your writing as reference:

> "Here are three paragraphs from my previous work that represent my voice:
>
> [Example 1]
> [Example 2]
> [Example 3]
>
> Write a new paragraph about [topic] in this same voice."

**What works:**
- Recent, representative samples
- Variety (not just your best paragraph three times)
- Clear, distinctive examples

**What doesn't work:**
- Too little sample (AI needs patterns to detect)
- Inconsistent samples (confuses the pattern)
- Over-edited samples (may not reflect natural voice)

### The Style Guide Approach

Explicitly describe your voice:

> "My writing style:
> - Short sentences. Punchy. Sometimes fragments.
> - British English spelling
> - Technical terms explained on first use, then used freely
> - Occasional dry humour, never sarcasm
> - Active voice strongly preferred
> - I use 'you' to address readers directly"

This works better for:
- Consistent application across long documents
- Collaboration with others
- When you can't easily find good samples

```
EXERCISE:

1. Typical sentence structure and length
2. Vocabulary preferences (formal/informal, jargon policy)
3. Tone characteristics
4. Any distinctive quirks or habits
5. Things you never do
```

## The Uncanny Valley of AI Voice

Even with good style guidance, AI writing can feel "off":

**Signs of AI-ish writing:**
- Every paragraph the same length
- Formulaic transitions ("Furthermore...", "In conclusion...")
- Hedging everywhere ("It's important to note that...")
- Lists where prose would be natural
- Relentless positivity or balance
- No genuine surprise or personality

**The test:** Would you be embarrassed if readers knew AI wrote this?

If yes, it needs more work.

## Voice Preservation Strategies

### Strategy 1: AI Drafts, You Rewrite

Use AI for structure and content; do a full voice pass yourself.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    DRAFT → VOICE PASS                        │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                             │
│  AI generates:     Structure, key points, rough prose       │
│        ↓                                                    │
│  You add:          Voice, personality, authentic expression │
│        ↓                                                    │
│  Result:           Your ideas, your voice, faster           │
│                                                             │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Effort:** High, but guaranteed authenticity.

### Strategy 2: Constrained Generation

Give AI strict constraints that force your style:

> "Write this in exactly 3 sentences. First sentence: setup (max 8 words). Second: the key point. Third: the implication, starting with 'Which means...'."

Constraints reduce AI's tendency toward generic patterns.

### Strategy 3: Iterative Refinement

Generate, then refine with specific voice feedback:

**Turn 1:** "Write a product description for this coffee maker"
**Turn 2:** "Too formal. Make it conversational — like talking to a friend"
**Turn 3:** "Better. But 'revolutionise your morning' is cliché. Be more specific and less hyperbolic"
**Turn 4:** "Good. Now shorter — this should be scannable"

Each iteration gets closer to voice.

### Strategy 4: The Blend

Write your own opening, let AI continue:

> "Here's my opening paragraph — continue in this voice:
>
> 'I didn't expect to love a spreadsheet. But here I am, three months into a relationship with a Google Sheet that tracks my sourdough starters, and I'm not ashamed to admit: it's changed my baking.'"

AI picks up voice cues from your lead.

```
QUIZ:
AI generates a blog post in your voice. It's technically correct but feels generic. What's the most effective next step?

* Accept it — readers won't notice
* Reject it and write from scratch
*! Identify specific phrases that feel "AI-ish" and give targeted feedback
* Add more examples to your style guide

FEEDBACK: Targeted feedback on specific issues is more effective than general complaints. Point to exact phrases and explain why they don't work.
```

## Consistency Across Long Works

For books, ongoing blogs, or brand content, voice must stay consistent:

### The Voice Document

Create a living reference:

```markdown
# [Project] Voice Guide

## Core Voice Attributes
- Authoritative but approachable
- Technical precision with accessibility
- Occasional humour, never at reader's expense

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| "Let's look at..." | "It is important to examine..." |
| "This breaks when..." | "Issues may arise if..." |
| Short paragraphs | Walls of text |
| Specific examples | Abstract generalities |

## Sample Phrases (Good)
- "Here's the thing:"
- "In practice, this means..."
- "Which sounds great until..."

## Sample Phrases (Avoid)
- "It's worth noting that..."
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "At the end of the day..."

## Reference Passages
[Include 2-3 paragraphs that exemplify the target voice]
```

Include this document in every AI prompt for the project.

### The Consistency Check

After generating content, ask AI to review for voice consistency:

> "Compare this new section to my voice guide. Flag any phrases or patterns that don't match."

This catches drift before it compounds.

## Collaborative Voice

When multiple people contribute to a shared voice (brand, publication, team blog):

**Challenges:**
- Individual voices bleed through
- AI defaults vary by user
- Style drift over time

**Solutions:**
- Shared voice document (everyone uses it)
- Central review for voice consistency
- Regular voice calibration ("Does this sound like us?")

## Voice and Authenticity

A deeper question: Is AI-assisted writing authentic?

**One perspective:** Voice is about *how* you express ideas. If AI helps execute your vision in your voice, it's still authentically yours.

**Another perspective:** True voice emerges from the struggle of writing. AI smooths away the friction that creates distinctiveness.

**A pragmatic middle:** Be honest with yourself about how much is you. Use AI where it helps; preserve what matters.

```
EXERCISE:
Take something you've written recently. Ask AI to rewrite it "in a more professional tone." Compare:

1. What changed?
2. What was lost?
3. What actually improved?
4. Which version sounds more like you?
```

## Key Takeaways

- Voice is what makes writing distinctively yours — diction, rhythm, tone, perspective
- AI defaults to generic; you must actively train it on your voice
- Use style references (examples) or style guides (descriptions) — or both
- Watch for "AI-ish" patterns: uniform structure, formulaic transitions, relentless hedging
- Strategies: draft→rewrite, constrained generation, iterative refinement, blending
- Create a voice document for consistency across long works
- Be honest about authenticity — use AI where it helps, preserve what matters
