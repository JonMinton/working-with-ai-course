# O4. Communication & Meetings

## The Communication Load

Knowledge workers spend enormous time on communication:
- Writing and responding to messages
- Preparing for and running meetings
- Following up after conversations
- Keeping stakeholders informed

AI can reduce the friction without reducing the quality.

## Email Efficiency

### The Morning Triage

> "Here are 15 emails I received overnight. For each:
> - Priority (high/medium/low/FYI only)
> - Action required (reply, task, delegate, none)
> - Suggested response (1 sentence)"

Get an overview before diving in.

### The Quick Reply

> "Reply to this email: confirm the meeting, ask for the agenda, note I'll bring the Q3 numbers."

Bullet points in, professional prose out.

### The Thoughtful Reply

> "This email is from my skip-level manager asking about project delays. Draft a reply that:
> - Acknowledges the concern
> - Explains the causes honestly but not defensively
> - Proposes concrete next steps
> - Keeps it under 150 words"

Specify tone and constraints for sensitive emails.

### The Follow-Up Sequence

> "I sent this proposal 5 days ago with no response. Draft a follow-up that:
> - Isn't pushy
> - References the original email
> - Makes it easy to respond
> - Offers a clear next step"

Follow-ups are awkward to write; AI handles them well.

```
QUIZ:
You receive an email with an unreasonable request from an important client. What's the best approach?

* Respond immediately to show responsiveness
* Ignore it and hope they forget
*! Draft a response with AI, then review carefully and possibly sleep on it before sending
* Forward to your manager without comment

FEEDBACK: Difficult emails benefit from AI drafting, but should never be sent without careful review. Important or sensitive messages often benefit from a cooling-off period before sending.
```

## Slack and Teams Messages

### The Channel Update

> "Write a brief channel update about [project]. Keep it scannable — bullets, not paragraphs. Highlight: what's done, what's next, any blockers."

Channel posts need to be skimmable.

### The DM That's Actually Important

> "I need to ask my colleague to take over a task because I'm overloaded. Make it clear this is a real request, not just venting, but don't sound desperate."

Tone matters in workplace messages.

### The Thread Summary

> "This Slack thread is 47 messages. Summarise: What was decided? What's still unresolved? Who's doing what?"

Long threads lose context; summaries restore it.

## Meeting Preparation

### Agenda Creation

> "I'm meeting with [person] about [topic] for 30 minutes. Create an agenda that:
> - Starts with a quick context-setter
> - Covers the 3 decisions we need to make
> - Leaves 5 minutes for next steps"

Good agendas make good meetings.

### Pre-Read Preparation

> "Here's a 20-page document I need to discuss in tomorrow's meeting. Summarise:
> - Key points I should know
> - Likely questions others will ask
> - Potential concerns to prepare for"

Show up prepared without reading everything.

### Stakeholder Briefing

> "I'm presenting to the executive team. They'll have 10 minutes and limited context. Create a one-page brief that:
> - Summarises the situation
> - States the recommendation
> - Anticipates their concerns
> - Has clear ask"

Match the format to the audience.

```
EXERCISE:
You have a meeting tomorrow with a difficult stakeholder about a project that's behind schedule.

Use AI to prepare:
1. A brief agenda (shared in advance)
2. Your key talking points
3. Likely objections and how to address them
4. A proposed path forward
```

## Meeting Notes and Follow-Up

### Real-Time to Structured

If you have rough notes from a meeting:

> "Here are my raw notes from today's meeting. Convert to structured minutes:
> - Attendees
> - Topics discussed
> - Decisions made
> - Action items (with owners and deadlines)"

### The Action Item Extract

> "From these meeting notes, extract only the action items. For each:
> - What needs to be done
> - Who's responsible
> - When it's due"

Action items get lost in discussion notes.

### The Follow-Up Email

> "Draft a follow-up email for this meeting. Include:
> - Thanks for attending
> - Summary of key decisions
> - Action items (who/what/when)
> - Next meeting date
> - Keep it brief"

Send within 24 hours while context is fresh.

## Status Updates and Reports

### The Weekly Update

> "Here's what happened this week on my projects. Write a status update for my manager:
> - Accomplishments
> - In progress
> - Blockers/risks
> - Next week's priorities"

Consistent format makes updates easy to write and read.

### The Stakeholder Update

> "Write a stakeholder update that:
> - Leads with outcomes, not activities
> - Uses their language (business impact, not technical details)
> - Is honest about challenges without being alarming
> - Has a clear 'what we need from you' if relevant"

Stakeholders care about different things than your team.

### The Exception Report

> "Nothing significant to report this week. Write a brief 'no news is good news' update that:
> - Confirms things are on track
> - Notes any minor items
> - Doesn't waste the reader's time"

Even uneventful periods need acknowledgment.

```
QUIZ:
Your project is behind schedule. How should AI help with the status update?

* Write it to make the situation sound better than it is
*! Help you frame the situation honestly with clear context and proposed solutions
* Skip the update until there's better news
* Generate excuses for the delay

FEEDBACK: AI helps you communicate clearly, not dishonestly. Bad news delivered well (with context, causes, and solutions) builds more trust than good news that later turns out to be wrong.
```

## Difficult Communications

### Delivering Bad News

> "I need to tell [person] that [bad news]. Help me:
> - Be direct but compassionate
> - Explain the reasons briefly
> - Focus on what happens next
> - Not over-apologise"

Bad news is hard to deliver; AI helps find the right tone.

### Saying No

> "I need to decline this request. The real reason is [X] but I can't say that directly. Draft a response that:
> - Is clearly a no, not a maybe
> - Offers an alternative if possible
> - Preserves the relationship
> - Doesn't over-explain"

"No" is a complete sentence, but context helps.

### Navigating Conflict

> "I'm in a disagreement with [person] about [topic]. I believe [X], they believe [Y]. Help me write a message that:
> - Acknowledges their perspective
> - Explains my reasoning
> - Proposes a way forward
> - Doesn't escalate"

De-escalation is a skill; AI can model it.

## Templates for Recurring Communication

### Build a Template Library

For messages you send regularly:

```markdown
# Template: Project Kickoff Email

Subject: [Project Name] — Kickoff Information

Hi [Team],

We're kicking off [Project Name] this [day/date].

**What:** [One sentence description]
**Why:** [Business context]
**Timeline:** [Key dates]
**Your role:** [What you need from them]

I'll send detailed information [when]. Questions? Reply to this email or grab me on Slack.

Looking forward to working with you on this.

[Your name]
```

Then: "Customise this template for [specific project]"

### When to Template vs. Generate Fresh

**Use templates for:**
- Routine communications with standard structure
- Messages where consistency matters
- Time-sensitive situations (faster to customise than create)

**Generate fresh for:**
- Unique situations
- Sensitive communications
- When personalisation matters most

## Key Takeaways

- Email: triage with AI, quick replies from bullets, careful review for sensitive messages
- Slack/Teams: keep channel posts scannable, summarise long threads
- Meeting prep: agendas, pre-read summaries, stakeholder briefs
- Meeting follow-up: structured minutes, extracted action items, prompt follow-up emails
- Status updates: consistent format, audience-appropriate framing, honest about challenges
- Difficult communications: AI helps with tone for bad news, declining, and de-escalation
- Build templates for recurring communications, generate fresh for unique situations
